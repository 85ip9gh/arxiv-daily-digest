QuoteBench: How Matched Scores Can Hide Command-Path Failures

Shangao Li, Yao Zhang, Volker Tresp and 1 others · cs.AI · abstract · pdf

Problem. Matched execution scores could not distinguish between command-generation errors and post-generation failures.

Approach. The authors introduced QuoteBench, which uses exact final-state validation across 56 one-shot tasks with an added parser to measure the boundary between generation and execution.

Result. Replaying the same reply through the added parser reduced success by 55.4 to 73.2 percentage points, and disclosure recovered 30.4 to 60.7 points for six configurations.

Why it matters. Researchers and developers of command-issuing agents should care because matched scores do not reflect intrinsic model properties and depend on deployment configurations.

Escaping at the interpolation point reproduces each replayed reply's raw-path outcome

Picked because: QuoteBench provides concrete evaluation of LLM coding agents by measuring command-path failures through exact final-state validation on real-world tasks, offering actionable insights for improving tool use reliability.

TraVEL: Trajectory-Guided Video Embedding Learning for Driving-Video Retrieval

Yi-Chung Chen, Philip Jacobson, Tom Lampo and 6 others · cs.CV · abstract · pdf

Problem. General-purpose models struggled to distinguish motion-centric events due to reliance on static scene context.

Approach. The authors fine-tuned a multimodal embedding model using ego-trajectory similarity in Group Relative Policy Optimization.

Result. TraVEL improved longitudinal and lateral mAP by 9.8 and 4.7 points at 2B, with gains of 7.2 and 1.5 points at 8B.

Why it matters. Researchers and developers working on driving-video retrieval should care because TraVEL improves motion-based search efficiency.

Experiments show that TraVEL improves motion-centric retrieval across model scales

Picked because: TraVEL introduces a trajectory-guided video embedding method for driving-video retrieval, offering a practical approach to efficiently search large driving logs with minimal auxiliary data and expert rules.

DFM Mimir v1: An Open HRM Delivering Frontier Performance at 1B Parameters Using Only Permissible Post-Training Data

Peter Schneider-Kamp, Jacob Nielsen, Gianluca Barmina and 2 others · cs.CL · abstract · pdf

Problem. Current large language model development relies on massive, often non-permissible datasets, creating a high barrier for researchers committed to open-source and ethically sourced data.

Approach. The authors trained Mimir v1, a 1-billion-parameter language model, using only permissible post-training data and a mixture of 161 datasets.

Result. Mimir v1 outperforms the original HRM-Text 1B and competes with larger frontier models like Qwen 3.5 4B and Gemma 4 E2B, tested across 20 benchmarks for English, Math & Code and Danish.

Why it matters. Researchers and developers interested in ethical and open-source models should care because Mimir v1 achieves frontier performance without non-permissible data.

Trained on a mixture of 161 datasets, Mimir v1 outperforms the original HRM-Text 1B and competes with larger frontier models like Qwen 3.5 4B and Gemma 4 E2B

Picked because: DFM Mimir v1 is a 1B-parameter open-source language model trained on permissible post-training data, delivering competitive performance and providing an accessible artifact for efficient inference on smaller, local models.
